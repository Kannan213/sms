Title: FX Trade Processing Architecture

Slide 2 – What Are We Trying to Achieve?
Heading: What Are We Trying to Achieve?
Intro text: The trade-acceptor service is the backbone of our FX trade distribution pipeline.
Four sections, each with a bold heading and description:

Single Entry Point: One reliable service receives all FX trades from upstream, removing ambiguity about where trades enter the platform
Data Integrity: Every trade is validated, enriched and persisted via stored procedure before anything downstream is notified
Decoupled Distribution: Downstream systems subscribe to trade-bus independently — the acceptor does not care who listens or how many consumers there are
Resilience by Design: No trade is silently lost. Failures are captured in middleware-resilience so support can replay after debugging the root cause


Slide 3 – Architecture Flow
Heading: Architecture Flow
Show a step-by-step pipeline with these 4 steps in order:
Step 1 – Inbound: Upstream system sends XML payload into a Solace inbound queue
Step 2 – Validate and Enrich: trade-acceptor validates against schema and business rules, enriches and transforms the trade. Raw XML is also archived to Amazon S3 at this point
Step 3 – Persist: Enriched trade is written to Trade Database via stored procedure, then the result set is retrieved
Step 4 – Build and Publish: Result set is converted to Protobuf message and published to trade-bus (Solace queue)
Below the pipeline, show three supporting capabilities:

S3 Archive: Raw inbound XML stored for audit and replay
middleware-resilience: Captures any process failure. Support team can replay the trade after debugging the root cause
Observability: Splunk and ELK for logging, Grafana for dashboards and alerts, Mimir for metrics storage, Tempo for distributed tracing


Slide 4 – trade-bus: Open Distribution
Heading: trade-bus — Open for Any Consumer
Intro: trade-bus is a Solace queue carrying Protobuf messages. Any service that needs FX trade data simply subscribes. No changes are ever needed in trade-acceptor.
Show trade-bus connecting to three current consumers:

adaptor-a → converts Protobuf and delivers to Downstream System A
adaptor-b → converts Protobuf and delivers to Downstream System B
adaptor-c → converts Protobuf and delivers to Downstream System C
Also show a fourth placeholder labelled "Future Consumer – just subscribe to trade-bus"
Four key points:
trade-acceptor publishes once — all consumers receive independently
Adding a new downstream system requires zero changes to trade-acceptor
Each adaptor is responsible for converting the Protobuf into the format its downstream expects
Protobuf is the shared contract — versioned, strongly typed, and efficient
